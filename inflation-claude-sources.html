A note on wording

The bakery, the one-coin and two-coin loaf, the speedometer, the foggy ship and the anchored boat are analogies and illustrations. The numbers 100 and 110, ten and four percent, five and seven percent, and the loaf and customer counts are illustrative, not statistics; only the 2008 hyperinflation estimate and the definition of hyperinflation are real figures, and they come from the IMF explainer. The consumer price index and the Federal Reserve details are about the United States; the IMF explainer is general. Economists weigh the causes of inflation differently and the sources say they can interact, so the video says "can" and "often". That policy can cost jobs is the video's own plain-language reading of the Federal Reserve statement (the two goals can conflict) and the IMF note that policymakers may keep activity low for a time; it is stated as a possibility. The reason the usual goal is not zero is the video's link between the IMF discussion of deflation risks and the Federal Reserve's two percent aim; the Federal Reserve's own explanation was not opened.
